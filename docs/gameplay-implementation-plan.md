# GTA MMMUT gameplay implementation plan

Build a living campus around three connected places. Deoria Road carries continuous traffic past the gates. A smaller stream of cars and a larger stream of bikes enter campus. At the cafeteria, students park, dismount, and join friends before heading back out. The player can do the same trip, follow it on the numbered campus map, and complete a checkpoint mission.

Start in the **B72 Training and Placement parking area** with one character and one hatchback. Use that small scene to verify the camera, movement, animation, vehicle transitions and stopping before adding traffic. This plan turns the earlier research into implementation milestones. [Gameplay systems research](research/gameplay-systems.md)

## Existing game and asset choices

The game already has camera modes, a rigged avatar, drivable cars, a moped and a bicycle. The vehicle controller supports acceleration, steering and braking, and the current traffic scene alternates two models on one main gate–T&P route. Deoria Road already has three lanes per direction, a median and a sidewalk across the gate aprons. Campus NPCs follow authored patrols or stay at benches, gardens and the gate. B21 has the cafeteria façade, forecourt and steps. The live map already projects campus geometry and the player onto an SVG. [Project baseline](../README.md) · [Deoria geometry](../packages/world-data/src/deoria-road.ts) · [Traffic controller](../apps/web/src/campus-traffic.ts) · [Pedestrians](../apps/web/src/campus-pedestrians.ts) · [Cafeteria scene](../apps/web/src/building-campus-landmarks.ts)

Use *San Andreas* as a reference for player-visible controls, camera feel, traffic and vehicle variety. The local installation gives access to the game for play. Publishing Rockstar cars, characters, animations or sound in the browser game requires separate reuse rights. Build the fleet and students from original or documented licensed assets. Record the source, author, license, derivative, scale, pivots, animation compatibility and LOD for each runtime file. Preserve existing model credits and review brand marks separately. ## Movement and vehicle implementation

- **Shared simulation.** Feed one input frame into each 60 Hz local simulation step. Keep character feet, chassis pose, seat ownership and checkpoint state in TypeScript. Have Babylon render from committed poses. Retain the current keyboard bindings. Clear held controls on blur or pause, and interpolate camera and model movement between ticks.
- **Character and camera.** Use a Havok capsule with walkable slope, curb height and standing clearance checks. Keep camera yaw independent of player heading. Sweep a camera sphere toward the desired eye, pull it in immediately at walls or canopy posts, then ease it out. Blend idle, walk, jog and sprint from resolved speed so pushing into a wall settles to idle. Use jump, fall and land states and preserve reduced motion and look sensitivity. Verify the pinned Babylon/Havok integration in the standalone scene. [Babylon character controller](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/physics/characterController/) · [Babylon shape casts](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/physics/shapeCast/)
- **Entry and exit.** Define approach, door, seat and exit anchors per vehicle. On **E**, reserve the seat, verify the path, play the approach and blend to the seat. Transfer controls, camera target and map pose together. Brake before exit and check ground support and capsule clearance on each side. If no safe exit exists, keep the driver seated and show the reason. Use mount and dismount anchors on bikes and a kickstand pose after parking.
- **Handling.** Begin with fixed-step, swept chassis movement and signed-speed steering. Apply braking before reversing and reduce maximum steering at higher speeds. Verify a four-contact Havok car prototype with reusable wheel ground probes. Apply spring force from wheel compression, damping from relative speed, and tire force limits from ground friction and normal load. Tune rebound, braking distance and curb behavior on a repeatable track. Use steering and lean for motorcycles with low-speed stabilization. Give bicycles separate speed and pedaling profiles. Lane-following ambient traffic can use lightweight kinematic poses with nearby collision proxies. [Babylon Havok integration](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/physics/havokPlugin/) · [Raycasts](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/physics/raycast/)
- **Drift and sound.** In the practice lot, give selected cars a handbrake input that briefly reduces rear traction. Tune slip, countersteer and recovery and retain normal service braking for campus driving. Use licensed or synthesized engine, motor, tire, brake and road layers. Derive pitch and gain from RPM, throttle, load and slip. Resume audio after a player gesture, honor mute and pool a small number of nearby spatial emitters. Test at idle, braking, acceleration and drift recovery. [Web Audio spatial sound](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio/#PannerNode)

## Traffic and pedestrian rules

1. **Deoria Road.** Give each lane a direction, allowed vehicle classes, speed, spawn boundary and connected junctions. Start with **85–95% of eligible arrivals continuing straight** and **5–15% turning into campus** as tuning values. Author the main gate movement from the adjacent carriageway. Use the existing median geometry and add new turning connections only when their layout is verified. Spawn outside the player's immediate view with enough room to merge. Sort cars by progress along each lane and derive safe following distance from speed and braking. Crosswalks, turning cars and the player's position use local spatial queries. Reserve conflict zones and avoid entering a junction that has no space on the far side.
2. **Campus roads.** Reuse the existing main gate, splitter and T&P route first. Add branches with known or clearly provisional access. Begin with a **60–75% two-wheeler share** among campus ambient trips and aim for **3–6 moving bikes and 2–4 moving cars** in the focus area during the first tuning pass. Bikes slow for walkers, wait at crossings and travel in their lane. Stalled routes release reservations and recover by rerouting or waiting at designated points.
3. **Cafeteria.** B21 is confirmed in the campus registry. Its exact bike approach and parking arrangement need on-site or photo confirmation. Use a short provisional connector from a validated campus lane and marked bays beside the forecourt for the prototype. A rider reserves a bay, slows, parks, dismounts and walks to a free chat position. Two or three students face each other and vary their idle and talk gestures before departing. A full rack diverts the rider or queues them in an authored waiting area. Keep the entry and footpath clear. [Campus registry](../packages/world-data/src/registry.ts) · [Road traces](../packages/world-data/src/campus-roads.ts)
4. **Pedestrian AI.** Use a separate graph for sidewalks and covered walkways with connections to doors, benches, parking and crossings. Route with A* and assign fictional schedules with travel, wait, cross, sit, chat and leave states. Use a spatial grid to see nearby actors and obstacles. Slow or sidestep within walkable width, take turns at narrow points and replan after a short delay. Share crossing reservations with traffic so a pedestrian and a turning vehicle agree on who goes first. Reuse pooled rigs near the player and update distant schedules at lower rates.

## Numbered map, checkpoints and missions

Build the map from the existing registry IDs and SVG world geometry. Keep the 1623 × 928 annotation coordinates and runtime transform. Show building numbers at useful zoom levels, offer keyboard-searchable building names, and show foot or vehicle routes from the same graphs used by AI. Keep image-up until true north is verified. The live marker uses the committed character or vehicle pose, with heading computed by projecting its forward direction. Refresh the marker independently of the static map and keep **M** for the full overlay. [Map UI](../apps/web/src/app.tsx) · [Coordinate transform](../packages/world-data/src/southern-entrance.ts)

Define safe respawn checkpoints separately from mission steps. Use stable IDs and trigger conditions such as location, mode, floor, minimum dwell time and completed prerequisites. The first mission teaches the B72 walk, drive and park loop. The second sends the player from T&P to the cafeteria by bike, verifies parking, then asks them to walk to B21. Save versioned checkpoint and mission IDs in IndexedDB. Write the current and previous save together, validate the record on load, and resume at a clear on-foot anchor. Keep the save small and support recovery from corrupt data, older schema versions and stale writes from another tab. [IndexedDB transactions](https://www.w3.org/TR/IndexedDB/#transaction-construct)

## Streaming, performance and multiplayer

- **Sector loading.** Partition the B72 lot, gate and forecourt, and cafeteria approach using shared boundaries. Keep a coarse road graph and map available across all sectors. Preload the next sector along the player's route, activate collision before arrival, and unload at a longer distance. Pin the sector containing the player, occupied vehicle or active mission anchor. Reuse Babylon asset containers and shared materials across sectors, and retry failed detail loads with a visible proxy and usable collision. [Babylon asset containers](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/importers/assetContainers)
- **Crowd and fleet budgets.** Start with **10–20 active NPCs in the first district**, then profile **roughly 12–24 nearby full rigs** with additional distant simplified actors. Update nearby AI at about 10–20 Hz and distant schedules at 1–5 Hz as a starting point. Interpolate visible movement. Hundreds of scheduled identities across campus should remain plausible when the player returns to a sector, with stable IDs and schedules. Group static instancing by sector, pool vehicle meshes and audio emitters, and stream detailed GLBs only where needed. [Babylon thin instances](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/mesh/copies/thinInstances/)
- **Benchmarks.** Capture the current frame and load baseline before implementation. Measure the same three-minute gate → T&P → cafeteria run at each density tier. Record frame and simulation p50/p95, query counts, near and distant actors, draw calls, transfer size, time to player control and memory after three boundary laps. Aim for p95 near 16.7 ms at 1080p medium on a named mid-range desktop GPU and under 33.3 ms on low as initial targets. The preproduction cold-load stretch is 100 MB. These are targets to measure. [Project quality targets](../GTA_MMMUT_Preproduction_Master_Pack_v01/docs/09_QUALITY_AND_ACCEPTANCE.md)
- **Shared rooms.** Keep command schemas, stable entity IDs and map revisions independent of rendering from the first ticket. In multiplayer, the Node/Colyseus room validates inputs, owns authoritative positions, seats, crossings and progression, and publishes tick and input acknowledgments. Interpolate remote actors and reconcile the local player. Use client-only simulation for distant scenery traffic that cannot interact with anyone. Once baseline replication is measured, send nearby entity state by sector. Test duplicate and out-of-order inputs, seat contention and reconnect at around 150 ms round-trip latency with simulated packet loss. Keep camera and HUD state local. [Colyseus rooms](https://docs.colyseus.io/room) · [State views](https://docs.colyseus.io/state/view)

## First implementation ticket

Build the standalone B72 lot using the current campus transform, one cleared avatar and an unbranded hatchback. Include the footpath, curb, wall corner, low overhang and marked parking bay. Instrument simulation ticks and frame time. First verify camera clearance, walking, sprinting, jumping, and stop/landing animation with Vitest and a Chrome walkthrough. Then add the door-to-seat transition and one-car parking loop in the same fixture. Use the measured result to establish the handling and camera behavior that later traffic and campus missions will depend on.
